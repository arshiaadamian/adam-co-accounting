Running a small business means wearing a lot of hats — and tax planning is one that many owners leave until the last minute. Here are five practical tips that can save you money and stress.

## 1. Separate Your Business and Personal Finances

If you're still using a single bank account for everything, open a dedicated business checking account today. This makes it dramatically easier to track deductible expenses and gives you a clean paper trail if you're ever audited.

## 2. Track Every Deductible Expense

Common deductions that small business owners miss:

- Home office (if you work from home)
- Vehicle mileage for business travel
- Professional development and education
- Software subscriptions and tools
- Health insurance premiums (for self-employed individuals)

Keep receipts, use an expense-tracking app, or let your accountant set up a system for you.

## 3. Make Quarterly Estimated Tax Payments

If you're self-employed, you're likely required to pay taxes quarterly rather than annually. Missing these deadlines can result in underpayment penalties. Your accountant can help you calculate the right amount to set aside each quarter.

## 4. Maximize Retirement Contributions

Contributing to a SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) reduces your taxable income dollar for dollar. These are some of the most powerful tools available to self-employed individuals — and contributions can often be made up until the tax filing deadline.

## 5. Don't Wait Until April

The best time to talk to your accountant is before the tax year ends. Year-end planning — adjusting income, timing deductions, reviewing your entity structure — can make a significant difference in what you owe.
